# 14 — TCS2 (Tick Collection Service)

**STATUS: DRAFT — design in discussion 2026-09-19/20. No code. Points only.**

## Purpose
- One system that listens to Dhan ticks and serves them to whatever needs them.

## Decisions locked
- **D1** — TCS2 and TFA are **mutually exclusive**. TFA's own code/WS path is
  untouched; it simply is not run.
- **D10** (2026-09-23) — **TCS2 is the only program that runs.** TFA is not run
  at all.
  - **Connections: 4 of 5** — one per instrument process (D9). See the budget
    in D9.
  - The per-connection cap **is** in our code:
    `DHAN_WS_MAX_INSTRUMENTS_PER_CONN = 5000` in
    `server/broker/adapters/dhan/constants.ts`, and `subscriptionManager.ts`
    budgets against it. Dhan signals a breach with disconnect code 804,
    "Instruments exceed limit". **475 is what we have proven live**; 5,000 is
    Dhan's documented figure.
  - Every instrument fits inside one connection, the largest (banknifty, ~1,459)
    using under a third of the 5,000.
- **D2** — TCS2 is **not** a TFA replacement. Its own jobs. On a TCS2 day,
  TFA's outputs (live features for SEA/blast) simply are not produced.
- **D4 — REVISED 2026-09-23: ALL FOUR instruments.** The earlier "NIFTY 50 only"
  is withdrawn (Partha). **One program, run once per instrument**, as in D9.
  What differs per instrument is config, not code — see D11.
  - Consequence: nothing goes dark on a TCS2 day. blast's crude paper runner
    keeps its data.
- **D5** — listen to the **WHOLE option chain**, every strike, both sides, on
  **every expiry that instrument has** (3 for nifty, 2 for the rest — D11).
  Not a moving band around the money.
  - Reason: a band has to follow price through the day, so a position built at a
    far strike is invisible until the market drifts there. Points 12 and 13 are
    about positioning moving BETWEEN strikes — a window that moves with price
    could mask the very thing being looked for.
  - Cost is fine: Dhan allows 5,000 contracts per connection; raw ticks are not
    stored, so there is no disk cost.
- **D7** — **TCS2 stands alone. It does not depend on the API server.**
  - Its own Dhan login and its own contract list (detailed scrip master).
    **No chain fetch at all** — see D12; the chain is built from ticks and IV is
    computed. Needs its own copy of the credentials.
  - Reason: 2026-09-18 the server went down at 09:59 and TFA lost the option
    chain for all four instruments while its own Dhan price feed kept running.
    The thing everything depends on must not itself depend on a program that is
    restarted regularly.
- **D3** — listens to **option, index, futures and India VIX** ticks.
  - options = primary for the 15 rules
  - index = price, strikes, settlement (arrives as price only — no volume/book)
  - futures = supporting; **and the only underlying for crude/gas (no MCX index)**
  - VIX = volatility regime — **NSE only**; no crude/gas equivalent exists

- **D11 — per-instrument capability table (2026-09-23).** Measured from our own
  recordings on 2026-09-22, not from documentation:

  | | nifty50 | banknifty | crudeoil | naturalgas |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | spot index | yes | yes | **none** | **none** |
  | futures | yes | yes | yes, **is** the underlying | yes, **is** the underlying |
  | India VIX | yes, real | same VIX, borrowed | **meaningless** | **meaningless** |
  | weekly options | yes (Tuesday) | **no, monthly only** | **no, monthly only** | **no, monthly only** |
  | chains to watch | 3 (week/month/next) | 2 (month/next) | 2 | 2 |
  | strikes / legs, nearest chain | 236 / 472 | 364 / 728 | 207 / 414 | 92 / 184 |
  | futures contracts watched | 2 (D19) | 2 (D19) | 2 (D19) | 2 (D19) |
  | strike spacing | 50 | 100 (500/300 far out) | 50 | 5 |

  - Proof banknifty has no weeklies: on 2026-09-22 the nearest nifty expiry was
    **that same day**, the nearest banknifty expiry **2026-09-29** (month end).
  - TFA writes a `*_vix_ticks` file next to crude and gas too (~207 KB/day). It
    is the same India VIX instrument; for MCX it carries no information. TCS2
    should not subscribe it for crude/gas.
  - **S3 (current week) does not exist for banknifty, crude or gas.** Spec 15's
    points that compare across week/month/next (13, and the weekly half of 11/12)
    reduce to two expiries for three of the four instruments.
  - Totals if all four run: ~4,070 option legs plus futures/index/VIX. Past the
    475 proven on one connection, so expect **one connection per instrument**
    (4 of the 5 slots) or a split within an instrument for banknifty.
  - On MCX the options are written on a **futures contract**, and option expiry
    is a few days **before** the futures expiry it belongs to (crude options
    2026-10-15 on OCT FUT 2026-10-19). Pair them from the detailed scrip master,
    not by assuming the dates match.
  - **T191 corrected:** TFA already pairs all four correctly. The real MCX bug is
    a **frozen `spotPrice` in the chain snapshots** — 324 crude snapshots on
    2026-09-22, one single value. TCS2 avoids it by taking spot from the futures
    tick stream (D12).

- **D12 — the option chain is BUILT FROM TICKS, not fetched (Partha 2026-09-23).**
  TCS2 never calls Dhan's option-chain API. It subscribes every leg and assembles
  the chain itself from the tick stream.
  - **This works — the ticks carry everything the chain shows except IV.**
    Verified in the 2026-09-22 recordings, each option tick has: `ltp`, `oi`,
    `high_oi`, `low_oi`, `volume`, `atp`, `bid`/`ask` + sizes, 5-level `depth`,
    `total_buy`/`total_sell`, `day_open/high/low/close`, `ltq`, `ltt`, `strike`,
    `opt_type`.
  - **What must be computed instead of received:**
    - **IV** — the only chain field not in the tick stream. Black-Scholes implied
      vol from premium, spot, strike, time to expiry, r. The BS code already
      exists in `claude_cohort`.
    - **OI change** — from our own previous snapshot, not from Dhan.
  - **What must come from the scrip master:** the strike list, each leg's security
    id, lot size, tick size, and the weekly/monthly flag. Use Dhan's **detailed**
    master `api-scrip-master-detailed.csv`, not the compact one the server uses —
    it adds `UNDERLYING_SECURITY_ID` (26000 NIFTY, 26009 BANKNIFTY, 294 CRUDEOIL,
    401 NATURALGAS) and `EXPIRY_FLAG` (M/W), so legs group by id instead of by
    parsing symbol strings.
  - **Spot comes from the tick stream too** — the index tick for nifty/banknifty,
    the futures tick for crude/gas. This sidesteps T191's frozen MCX spot.
  - Why it matters beyond independence: a fetched chain is a snapshot every N
    minutes; a built chain is current on every tick, which is what points 11 and
    12 need.

- **D14 — total isolation between instrument processes (Partha 2026-09-23).**
  The tick analyser for an instrument runs **inside that instrument's process,
  alone**. No process shares ticks, chain state or analyser state with another.
  - **Shared code is fine; shared state is not.** All four run the same modules,
    imported as a library. What is forbidden is a common store, queue, cache or
    analyser instance that two instrument processes both touch.
  - Each process owns its own chain, its own rolling state, its own files, its
    own config.
  - **India VIX is subscribed twice** — once by nifty50, once by banknifty — on
    two different connections, rather than fetched once and shared. That is the
    rule working as intended: 1 leg each out of 5,000. TFA already does this,
    writing four separate `*_vix_ticks` files.
  - **What this costs, stated plainly:** nothing cross-instrument can be computed
    inside TCS2 — no nifty-vs-banknifty divergence, no crude-vs-gas correlation,
    no "NSE is risk-off so treat the MCX signal differently". If we want those
    later they belong in a **downstream consumer** reading the four processes'
    outputs, never in a shared analyser. Worth it: isolation is what keeps one
    instrument's bad day from taking the other three with it, which is exactly
    what T186 and the 2026-09-18 corruption did.

- **D15 — each process has its own screen (Partha 2026-09-23).** One instrument =
  one process = one screen. Four processes, four screens, as the per-instrument
  chart windows already work.
  - **This dissolves the "how do consumers get the data" question for the screen.**
    Nothing has to be delivered anywhere: the screen that shows an instrument is
    attached to the process that holds that instrument's ticks, chain and
    analyser state. No files to tail, no sockets, no shared store, and D14 is
    kept without effort.
  - **Consequence for spec 12:** today's screen is one window with a 2x2 grid,
    one quadrant per instrument. That becomes **four windows, each showing one
    instrument full-screen** — which is what spec 15 §5.7 already drew for nifty
    (rules panel, decision box, strike ladder, levels/migration). The four
    windows can sit on different monitors.
  - **Settled by D16: the screen runs inside the process.**
  - Job 3 ("share live ticks with other programs") therefore no longer has the
    screen as its driver. What remains of it is the SEA/blast question below.

- **D16 — the screen runs INSIDE the instrument process (Partha 2026-09-23).**
  One process per instrument, containing feed, chain, analyser, recorder and
  screen. Not eight processes.
  - Claude recommended a separate screen process; Partha chose same-process.
    Recorded so the reasoning is not relitigated.
  - **The arrangement that makes this safe, and it is not optional:**
    - **Tkinter on the MAIN thread** (required on Windows), **ticks and the
      recorder on worker threads.** That way a slow or frozen redraw cannot stop
      the feed — a blocked Tk mainloop releases the GIL, so the workers keep
      running.
    - The GUI **never touches the tick path**. It reads an immutable snapshot on
      a timer; the tick thread publishes snapshots and never waits on the GUI.
    - Bounded queues. **If the GUI falls behind, GUI updates are dropped —
      never ticks, never writes.**
    - The recorder gets its own thread, so a slow disk cannot stall the feed
      either.
  - **A heartbeat per thread, reported independently.** The feed, recorder and
    GUI each prove they are alive on their own. The failure this guards against
    is precisely T186 and 2026-09-18: a screen that looks fine while the
    recording behind it stopped hours ago.
  - **Accepted risk:** a hard crash in the GUI takes the feed and recorder with
    it, which would not happen with two processes. The heartbeats make it loud
    rather than silent, which is the part that actually cost us before.

- **D17 — health is shown ON SCREEN only; Telegram comes later (Partha 2026-09-23).**
  Each window shows its own process's three heartbeats (feed, recorder, GUI).
  No alerting to yow-partha in v1.
  - Claude recommended screen + Telegram; Partha chose screen only for now.
  - **Build it so Telegram is a subscriber, not a rewrite.** Health is a small
    structured object the process updates — per-thread last-beat, tick rate,
    last write, current staleness. The screen is one consumer of it. Adding a
    Telegram consumer later must not mean touching the health logic.
  - **Also write the health object to a file each interval**, even in v1. It
    costs nothing, and it is the difference between knowing *when* a process
    stopped and guessing. T186 is still unsolved partly because we have no such
    record for 2026-09-18.
  - **The gap this leaves, stated plainly:** an unattended stop goes unnoticed.
    MCX runs to 23:30 and NSE processes idle after 15:30; nobody is watching a
    screen then. On 2026-09-18 the recorder stopped at 10:00 and it took 2.5
    hours to notice *with* someone at the desk. So the screen must make
    staleness impossible to miss rather than subtle — the existing 60-second
    stale rule and last-updated clock in `market_screen` are the right shape,
    and the health row belongs at the top of the window, not the bottom.

- **D18 — scheduled tasks start them, the launcher inspects and restarts
  (Partha 2026-09-23).** Same split that already works for yow-partha: a
  Windows task gets it running without anyone at the desk, and the launcher is
  how it is watched and controlled afterwards.
  - **Four tasks**, named like the existing ones
    (`Lubas-YowPartha-Daily`, `Lubas-CB2-Tracker-Daily`):

    **All four start at 08:54** (Partha 2026-09-23), matching the existing
    `Lubas-YowPartha-Daily` 08:55 slot so the machine is woken once.

    | task | instrument | legs | market opens | warm-up | runs until |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | `Lubas-TCS2-Crudeoil-Daily` | crudeoil | 829 | 09:00 | 6 min | 23:30 |
    | `Lubas-TCS2-Naturalgas-Daily` | naturalgas | 369 | 09:00 | 6 min | 23:30 |
    | `Lubas-TCS2-Nifty50-Daily` | nifty50 | 1,419 | 09:15 | 21 min | 15:30 |
    | `Lubas-TCS2-Banknifty-Daily` | banknifty | 1,459 | 09:15 | 21 min | 15:30 |

  - **The warm-up fits, and it falls the right way round.** Each process must
    download the detailed scrip master (~35 MB), build its strike list and
    subscribe its legs at 100 instruments per message — 15 messages for
    banknifty. The two with only 6 minutes are the **smallest** (829 and 369
    legs, 9 and 4 messages); the two large ones get 21 minutes.
  - **One shared scrip-master file, downloaded once.** Four simultaneous 35 MB
    downloads at 08:54 is waste, and the file is identical for all four. A
    read-only cached file on disk is **not** a D14 violation — D14 bans shared
    analyser and tick state, not reading the same config. Rule: use the cached
    copy if it is less than 24 hours old, otherwise download. First process up
    downloads; the rest read.
  - **Launcher gets a TCS2 section:** the four processes with their D17 health,
    and start / stop / restart per instrument. Restarting one must not touch the
    other three (D14).
  - **Stopping is the unsolved half.** NSE processes should stop after 15:30 and
    MCX after 23:30, but *how* they stop depends on the deferred crash-safe
    recording decision — a graceful stop that closes the file cleanly is a
    different thing from being killed by the scheduler. **Do not wire an
    automatic stop until that is settled.** A mid-session restart is what
    corrupted the recordings on 2026-09-18.

- **D19 — always watch CURRENT and NEXT, plus the weekly where one exists
  (Partha 2026-09-23).** One rule covering both futures and options, applied per
  instrument from the scrip master's `EXPIRY_FLAG`, never assumed.
  - **Futures: current + next month, always.** Not only during rollover week.
    In the days before expiry volume drains from the near contract into the next,
    so flow read from the near one alone thins out exactly when it is being
    relied on. Cost is one extra instrument; the roll itself is also visible,
    which is information.
  - **Options: current + next, plus the current week for NIFTY only.**
  - **Weekly exists for NIFTY alone.** Confirmed from Dhan's detailed scrip
    master `EXPIRY_FLAG`: `NIFTY|M` and `NIFTY|W`, but `BANKNIFTY|M`,
    `CRUDEOIL|M`, `NATURALGAS|M` with no W row anywhere. Independent of the
    2026-09-22 recording evidence in D11, and agreeing with it.
  - **Measured leg counts** (Dhan detailed master, 2026-09-23 — note these are
    real per-expiry counts, not one count repeated):

    | process | expiries watched | option legs | + | total |
    |---|---|---|---|---|
    | nifty50 | week 10-06 (460), month 09-29 (536), next 10-27 (488) | 1,484 | index, 2 futures, VIX | 1,488 |
    | banknifty | month 09-29 (728), next 10-27 (710) | 1,438 | index, 2 futures, VIX | 1,442 |
    | crudeoil | month 10-15 (414), next 11-17 (370) | 784 | 2 futures | 786 |
    | naturalgas | month 09-23 (184), next 10-23 (170) | 354 | 2 futures | 356 |
    | | | **4,060** | | **4,072** |

  - Largest process 1,488 of 5,000 — D13 holds, one connection each, four of the
    five account slots.
  - **NIFTY's monthly expiry is also a weekly** (last Tuesday, e.g. 2026-09-29
    carries flag M). On those days "current week" and "current month" are the
    same chain and must not be subscribed twice.

- **D20 — raw tick retention: delete the oldest days (Partha 2026-09-23).**
  Confirms kind **A** of the record list. ~1 GB/day for all four, 330 GB free,
  so roughly **a year** before anything is dropped. Revisit in six months rather
  than engineering for it now.
  - Rejected: **thinning old days to near-the-money strikes.** It would save a
    lot — 93% of ticks are within +/-40 strikes — but it deletes exactly the
    far-strike positioning that points 11 and 12 exist to find, so the data
    would be thinnest where the question is hardest.
  - Rejected: **moving old days to an external disk.** A manual step that gets
    forgotten, and a silent gap is what T185 already cost us.
  - **Deletion must be deliberate and logged**, not a cleanup script nobody
    watches. We have already lost days without noticing (T185: 16 corrupt
    recordings, 12 on past MCX days losing 45-99.9%); the second time it must be
    because we chose to.

- **D21 — the chain updates on EVERY tick, for EVERY leg, and is persisted
  (Partha 2026-09-23).** Settles kind **B**.
  - **No clock-based snapshot.** The 15-minute snapshot proposed in D6 is
    withdrawn, and so is any 3-minute or on-OI-change variant. The chain is
    current as of the last tick received, always.
  - **Never built from the API option-chain snapshot.** Those carry stale data —
    proven in T191, where MCX `spotPrice` was frozen at a single value across
    324 snapshots in one day while the futures moved 235 points.
  - **Persisted to the DB, not only held in memory.** Two shapes, because they
    answer different questions:
    - **current state** — one document per leg, upserted as ticks arrive. Bounded
      at ~4,060 documents, does not grow. Answers "what is the chain right now".
    - **history** — append-only rows, the multi-day positioning story that
      points 5-8, 11, 12 and 13 read. Kept until the contract expires.
  - **DB write rate — settled by D23/D24:** memory updates every tick; the DB is
    written in batches every few seconds, and history gets a row per OI change
    rather than per tick.

- **D22 — do not trust our own recordings as design evidence; confirm with Dhan
  (Partha 2026-09-23).** Recent days are unreliable, so any fact taken from
  stored files is provisional until checked against Dhan directly.
  - **Demonstrated the same day this was written.** `data/raw/2026-09-22/metadata.json`
    records `nifty50 underlying_security_id: 13` and `crudeoil: 486502`, while
    the ticks in the very same folder carry **68407** (NIFTY SEP FUT) and
    **569900** (CRUDEOIL OCT FUT). Neither stored id matches what was on the wire.
  - Already-known reasons to distrust: T185 (16 of 326 recordings corrupt, 12
    losing 45-99.9% of the day), T186 (recorder stopped silently at 10:00 on
    2026-09-18), T191 (frozen MCX spot in chain snapshots).
  - **Applies to design decisions, not to backtests.** Raw tick *contents* remain
    the best evidence we have and are what the flow study and backtests ran on.
    What must be confirmed with Dhan is **coverage and identity** — which
    instruments, which ids, how many legs, whether a day is complete.
  - **Standing consequence:** a measurement quoted from our recordings must say
    so, and must not become a design fact until Dhan confirms it. The leg counts
    in D19 came from Dhan's detailed scrip master and are safe; the
    "192 legs ticked on 2026-09-22" figure is **not** and needs checking.

- **D23 — kind B is stored in THREE tiers (Partha 2026-09-23).**

  | tier | what | grows | answers |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **Now** | one row per leg, **overwritten** on ticks | no, bounded ~4,060 | what is the chain right now |
  | **Intraday** | **a new row for every OI change, every strike** | ~720,000 rows/day, **kept 7 days** | how positioning moved *during* today |
  | **End of day** | **one row per strike per day** | ~4,060 rows/day | how positioning built over weeks |

  - **Why intraday is kept at all:** to measure intraday position change — OI
    piling into a strike *now*, not only by the closing bell.
  - **Why end-of-day is separate rather than derived:** it is the multi-day
    story, it is ~175x smaller, and it must stay readable long after the
    intraday detail is discarded.
  - **Why not simply overwrite one row:** an update is a photo, a new row is a
    timeline. 5,000,000 OI today only means something next to 2,000,000 last
    week, and overwriting erases exactly the multi-day build-up this system
    exists to see.
  - **Batched, not per-change.** Changes are collected and written every few
    seconds in one bulk call.
  - **Volume measured:** ~118 OI changes per leg per day on nifty, about one
    every 3 minutes; the busiest legs all hit exactly 118, so Dhan appears to
    publish OI on a fixed clock rather than per trade. **From our own
    recordings, so D22 applies: confirm with Dhan.** That gives ~720,000
    intraday rows/day across four instruments = **17.6 MB/day**, needing ~32
    writes/sec.
  - **Intraday tier retention: 7 days (Partha 2026-09-23).** ~125 MB total
    instead of ~6.4 GB/year.
    - Safe because every OI value is already inside kind A, so the intraday tier
      can be rebuilt **exactly**, any time. It is a convenience copy, not the
      only copy.
    - 7 days covers reviewing the week and debugging a recent signal, which is
      the only thing the fine detail is actually used for. The day-by-day story
      lives permanently in kind C (D29) and tier 3.
    - The cost of going back further is ~2 minutes per instrument per day to
      rebuild from raw ticks — acceptable for a study, unacceptable as a daily
      convenience, which is exactly the line this draws.

- **D24 — MongoDB: time-series for history, normal collection for Now
  (2026-09-23). Chosen on measurement, not on precedent.**
  Benchmarked locally on Mongo 7.0.9, throwaway DB, 200,000 rows:

  | | write rate | on-disk all-in | query one leg's history |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **time-series** | 38,056 rows/sec | **24.5 bytes/row** | **6 ms, no index** |
  | normal collection | 130,340 rows/sec | 69.3 bytes/row (incl. 4.2 MB index) | 11 ms, *with* index |
  | Parquet (zstd) | n/a | 15.5 bytes/row | static file, not live |

  - Time-series is **2.8x smaller all-in** and answers the query we care about
    faster **without** an index. The plain collection's only win is 3.4x faster
    inserts, which is worthless at ~32 writes/sec against 38,000/sec capacity —
    **0.08% utilisation**.
  - **Now-tier upserts: 13,102/sec; a full 1,484-leg flush takes 113 ms**, so
    flushing every 5 s is a 2% duty cycle on a worker thread.
  - Config: timeField t, metaField {sid, inst, strike, side, exp}, granularity
    minutes. Same shape as the existing ribbon_geometry collection.
  - **Limits, flagged before building:** time-series collections accept **no
    unique index**, so a restart or replay can duplicate rows — history writes
    must be idempotent by design. Their TTL is a fixed duration and cannot
    express "until this contract expires", so retention stays a deliberate job
    (consistent with D20).
  - Parquet is smaller still and is the right shape for an end-of-day analysis
    export, addable any time because kind A makes it reproducible.
  - *Method note: the first benchmark run reported 0.0 MB because WiredTiger had
    not checkpointed. The numbers above are after a forced fsync.*

- **D25 — what one chain row holds (2026-09-23).** One row = one strike, one side.
  - **Identity:** security id, instrument, strike, CE/PE, expiry
  - **From the tick:** LTP, ATP, OI, day high/low OI, volume, LTQ, bid, ask,
    bid size, ask size, 5-level depth, total buy/sell orders, day OHLC,
    exchange timestamp, our receive timestamp
  - **Calculated by us:** IV, delta, gamma, theta, vega, OI change, spread
  - **Per chain, not per row:** spot, futures price, futures basis, max pain,
    PCR, ATM strike, ATM straddle price, biggest call wall, biggest put wall
  - **Volume arrives in the tick — it is NOT calculated.** Only IV and the
    Greeks are, because Dhan sends neither.
  - **Speed measured:** whole 1,484-leg chain IV solve **14 ms** (71x/sec), all
    Greeks **0.71 ms**. Not a constraint.
  - **Use Black-76, not plain Black-Scholes, for MCX** — those options are
    written on futures (D11).
  - **Known gap:** the trial solver missed on far strikes (max IV error 0.17).
    Newton alone is not enough; a bisection fallback is required.
  - Existing code takes IV as an *input* (blast_model/greeks.py,
    claude_cohort/option_study.py, tick_feature_agent dealer_hedging_columnar.py)
    and are **reference only, not imports** (D27).
    **No implied-vol solver exists anywhere yet — that is new work.**

- **D26 — expiry rollover happens at STARTUP, never mid-session (2026-09-23).**
  - Each process restarts at 08:54 (D18) and re-reads the scrip master, so it
    always picks today's correct contracts. Nothing switches while the market is
    open — a mid-session change is what corrupted the recordings on 2026-09-18.
  - On a rollover day: yesterday's *next* becomes *current*, a new *next* is
    added, the expired one is dropped.
  - **NIFTY** rolls a weekly every Tuesday; **bank, crude, gas** roll monthly.
    **NIFTY's last-Tuesday weekly IS the monthly** — same chain, must not be
    subscribed twice.
  - **Futures** are always two contracts (D19), so the roll is visible as it
    happens.
  - **MCX gap:** crude options expire 2026-10-15 but crude futures live to
    2026-10-19 — the options die first and the futures keeps ticking four more
    days.
  - The expired contract stops being written but **keeps everything already
    stored**, so its full life stays readable. That is the point of history.

- **D27 — TCS2 is fully self-contained. It does NOT depend on `claude_cohort`
  (Partha 2026-09-23).** Extends D7 (no API server) and D14 (no cross-process
  sharing) to code as well: TCS2 imports nothing from the research packages.
  - `claude_cohort` (spec 13) is an **offline research and backtest** package.
    TCS2 is a **live** service. They must be able to change independently.
  - **TCS2 owns its own copy of everything it needs**, including:
    - trade-side classification, order-flow delta, cumulative delta, pressure,
      absorption, exhaustion, depth imbalance, liquidity removal
    - Black-76 / Black-Scholes, the implied-vol solver, the Greeks
    - gzip reading and writing
  - **The logic is proven, but the code is new.** `claude_cohort/flow.py` and
    `blast_model/greeks.py` are *reference implementations to copy the rules
    from*, not modules to import. Earlier wording in this spec suggesting flow.py
    "moves into TCS2 as-is" is withdrawn.
  - **The cost, stated plainly:** two copies of the same rule can drift, and a
    drift would mean the live screen and an offline backtest disagree while both
    look correct. **Mitigation: TCS2 is the source of truth for live behaviour,
    and agreement with the research code is enforced by a test, never by an
    import.** TFA already does this — `classify()` was written to match TFA's
    `underlying_trade_direction` exactly, by test rather than by shared code.

- **D28 — kind D is IN: record what the analyser said, from day one
  (Partha 2026-09-23).** Every minute, per instrument: the 25 points' scores and
  any TRADE / NO TRADE verdict with its reason. ~1 MB/day, rolling ~60 days.
  - **Why this one cannot be rebuilt from kind A, unlike tiers 2 and 3.** Chain
    state is *fact* — raw ticks regenerate it exactly. What the analyser *said*
    is an opinion at a moment. Re-running a changed analyser over old ticks
    yields the **new** answer, not the one that was actually on screen. Kind D is
    the only recording that is unrecoverable once lost.
  - **What it buys:** scoring the 25 points after ~60 live days; answering "it
    said 80 last Tuesday — was it right?"; catching a point that fires often and
    never works.
  - **Precedent for why it matters:** the 15 flow rules were only proven useless
    because the data existed to test them over 77 days and 26,671 decision
    points. Without an equivalent record, the 25 points cannot be judged at all,
    and §5.4 of spec 15 (scores describe, never predict, until measured) becomes
    unenforceable.
  - **Write it even when the verdict is NO TRADE.** The rejected setups are half
    the evidence.

- **D29 — kind C is IN, and the daily record is LOCKED (Partha 2026-09-23).**
  One write per instrument per day, rolling ~20 days for the reference parts and
  kept with tier 3 for the per-strike parts. A few KB plus ~4,000 strike rows.

  **1. Per strike, per side, per expiry**
  - OI: open, close, high, low, **change vs yesterday**
  - Option price: open, high, low, close
  - Volume, number of trades, average trade size
  - **Net aggressive buy vs sell volume** — who built it, buyers or writers.
    Without this, "OI up" is ambiguous: new longs and new writing look identical.
  - Premium traded (money committed, not just contracts)
  - Closing IV, delta, gamma
  - Average bid-ask spread — was the strike even tradeable
  - Distance from spot at close

  **2. Per expiry** (week / month / next, each separately)
  - Total call OI, put OI, **OI PCR**
  - Total call volume, put volume, **volume PCR** (a different signal from OI PCR)
  - Max pain at open and at close
  - ATM straddle at open and at close
  - ATM IV at open and at close — IV crush or expansion over the day
  - IV skew at close (put side vs call side)
  - Biggest call wall, biggest put wall
  - **Net delta-weighted flow for the day** — the single best "who won today"
    number; `claude_cohort/option_study.py` has the formula as reference (D27:
    copy, never import)
  - Days to expiry

  **3. Across expiries**
  - Which expiry took the flow today (week vs month vs next)
  - This is the raw input for expiry migration, spec 15 point 13

  **4. Futures** — both contracts (D19)
  - OHLC, VWAP, volume
  - **Futures OI: open, close, change.** Price up with OI up is a real long
    build-up; price up with OI down is only short covering and usually fades.
    This alone can flip a buy into a no-trade.
  - Basis at open and at close

  **5. Underlying / index**
  - OHLC, VWAP, day range
  - Realised volatility for the day
  - VIX open and close (**NSE only** — D11)
  - Gap versus yesterday's close
  - **Normal volume by time of day**, so "3x normal at 10:30" is measurable

  **6. Data quality stamp**
  - First tick time, last tick time, tick count, gaps detected, any stall
  - **A complete / partial / suspect flag per instrument per day**
  - Purpose: let a later study drop bad days automatically instead of silently
    averaging them in. This is exactly what went wrong when blast's crude dataset
    held 2026-08-21 with **2 rows** and nothing noticed (T185).

  **Why kind C exists at all:** at 09:15 the process has no history for today, so
  without it every morning starts blind. It is entirely rebuildable from kind A,
  but rebuilding means reading ~20 days of 1 GB files at 08:54 — so this is a
  startup cache, not new information.

  **What it buys at the live moment** (the argument, so it is not reopened):
  history is what turns a live number into a judgement. "23,500 CE has 5 million
  OI" means nothing on its own; that the wall has grown five days straight means
  it is defended, and that it appeared this morning means it is weak. Same
  number, opposite conclusion.

  **Standing caveat:** all of the above is **evidence to judge, not a proven
  signal**. The 15 flow rules looked equally sensible and failed over 77 days and
  26,671 decision points. Kind D (D28) exists so these get scored rather than
  believed.

## Storage — D6 positioning lifecycle (2026-09-23)
- **Capture positioning for ALL strikes, ALL three expiries, from the contract's
  START and continuing until it expires / rolls over.** Not a rolling window.
- Why: points 5-8, 11, 12, 13 are about big players building over days and moving
  between strikes and expiries. That story only exists if each contract's whole
  life is kept.
- What: chain snapshot rows — OI, OI change, premium, volume, IV per strike per
  side — at end of day plus every 15 min intraday.
- Size: ~1,400 rows per snapshot x 25 snapshots/day = a few MB/day. Raw ticks
  were 2.2 GB/day.
- Two ways to get "since start":
  1. forward from the day TCS2 starts
  2. **backfill from Dhan's historical API — TESTED 2026-09-23:**
     - **works, including for a contract that expired yesterday.** 23300 CE
       (expiry 09-22) returned its whole life: OI 65 on 09-01 -> 3,889,340 on 09-21
     - **old expiries return nothing** - a 2026-06-16 contract gave 0 candles.
       Dhan keeps recent expiries only; the cutoff is not yet found
     - **intraday 5-min candles carry OI too** - 231 candles over 09-18..09-22,
       better than daily
     - **BUT the two endpoints disagree.** For 23300 CE on 09-18, against our own
       recorded chain snapshot:
       | source | OI |
       |---|---|
       | our recording 09:15 -> close | 6,604,520 -> 8,520,590 |
       | Dhan intraday history | 8,061,170 |
       | Dhan daily history | 3,889,340, *identical on 09-21 and 09-22* |
       Intraday is in the right range; daily is less than half and repeats
       across days - looks stale. **Validate against our 81 days of recorded
       snapshots before relying on either.** See T189.
- We already hold **81 days of near-expiry chain snapshots** from TFA; those can
  be imported so past weeklies keep their final-week history.

## Storage — D8 raw ticks ARE stored (2026-09-23, reversing the earlier "live only")
- **TCS2 stores raw ticks.** Reason: with the analysis inside TCS2 and no
  recording, a change to the analysis could never be tested - there would be
  nothing to replay it against. Everything useful found this month (the flow
  study, the backtests, the futures-vs-options comparison) came from raw ticks
  being on disk.
- Size, **all four instruments**: measured 2026-09-22 with one chain each,
  compressed option ticks were nifty 174 MB, banknifty 144 MB, crude 131 MB,
  gas 29 MB = **478 MB/day**. Month and next-month chains trade far less, so
  adding them lands near **1 GB/day**. 330 GB free -> roughly **1 year**.
- **Must not repeat T185/T186:** a recorder heartbeat so a silent stop is caught,
  and files that survive a crash. `_shared/gz_reader.py` already reads past a
  corrupt member; the writing side needs the same care.

## D9 — one process per instrument, analysis INSIDE (2026-09-23, reaffirmed)
- **Confirmed by Partha 2026-09-23: each instrument runs in its own process.**
  Four processes: nifty50, banknifty, crudeoil, naturalgas. Same program, four
  configs.
- Matches TFA's proven shape; the launcher already handles per-instrument processes.
- Points 12/13 compare strikes and expiries, so keeping all of one instrument's
  contracts in one program beats shipping state between two.

**Connection and instrument budget** (legs from D11; cap 5,000/connection):

| process | chains | option legs | plus | total | connections |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| nifty50 | 3 | 1,416 | index + futures + VIX | 1,419 | 1 |
| banknifty | 2 | 1,456 | index + futures + VIX | 1,459 | 1 |
| crudeoil | 2 | 828 | futures | 829 | 1 |
| naturalgas | 2 | 368 | futures | 369 | 1 |
| | | **4,068** | | **4,076** | **4 of 5** |

**D13 — one connection per process, always (Partha 2026-09-23).** A process
never opens a second connection. It does not need to: 5,000 securities fit on
one, and the largest instrument asks for 1,459. This is a design rule, not a
budget that gets recalculated — if an instrument ever looks like it needs a
second connection, that is a bug or a wrong strike list, not a reason to split.
One of the five account slots stays spare.

**Why separate processes matter here beyond convention:**
- **Different market hours.** NSE runs 09:15-15:30, MCX 09:00-23:30. One process
  per instrument means crude and gas keep running after the NSE pair have shut
  down, instead of one long-lived process spanning both.
- **Independent restart.** A restart to fix one instrument does not interrupt the
  other three. T186 (recorder stopped silently) and the 2026-09-18 corruption
  both came from a process-wide event.
- **Isolation of a bad day.** A crash or a corrupt write is contained to one
  instrument's files.

- **Condition:** thresholds and parameters live in config the program re-reads
  while running, so tuning does not restart the feed. A mid-session restart is
  what corrupted the recordings on 2026-09-18.

## What gets recorded — five kinds (2026-09-23)

| | what | written | size/day, all four | kept | status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Raw ticks** — every tick of every subscribed leg, as received | continuously | ~1 GB | ~1 year, oldest deleted | **SETTLED D8 + D20** |
| **B** | **Chain state** — per strike per side: OI, dOI, premium, volume, IV, Greeks, bid/ask | **every tick, every leg** | ~17.6 MB | Now + intraday + end-of-day (D23) | **SETTLED D21, D23-D26** |
| **C** | **Daily record** — 6 parts: per strike, per expiry, across expiries, futures, underlying, data-quality stamp | once at EOD | KB + ~4,000 rows | ~20 days reference, per-strike with tier 3 | **SETTLED D29 — IN** |
| **D** | **What the analyser said** — the 25 points' output, and any verdict | every minute | ~1 MB | rolling ~60 days | **SETTLED D28 — IN** |
| **E** | **Health** — per-thread heartbeat, tick rate, last write, staleness | every interval | KB | rolling ~90 days | proposed (D17) |

**Not recorded: anything recomputable from A.** No derived features, no
intermediate flow state — being able to recompute them later with better code is
what keeping raw ticks buys.

**Why A is worth its gigabyte a day** (the argument, so it is not reopened):
1. Without it no analyser change can be tested — you would wait a live day per
   change and never compare two versions on the same data.
2. Everything found this month came from ticks on disk: the flow study over
   26,671 decision points across 77 days, the 78-day backtests with real
   per-contract fills, the 80-day futures-vs-options comparison, and the lot
   sizes settled from the GCD of `ltq` after the repo disagreed with itself.
3. Analyser bugs are only findable backwards. Pricing held trades off the ATM
   *slot* rather than a fixed strike made 44 stop-losses net +Rs 3,391; the ATM
   strike moved 434 times in one day. It was caught only by rebuilding
   per-contract books from raw ticks — without them that backtest would have
   looked profitable and been traded.
4. Ticks cannot be re-obtained. Dhan's history gives 5-minute candles with OI,
   not bid/ask/depth/trade side — exactly the fields the 15 rules need.
